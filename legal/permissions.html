Permissions — Grid Racer

Effective date: 2026-10-02

Published at: https://piepgras.info/legal/grid-racer/permissions/

Every permission the installed app holds, why it is there, and what happens if you refuse it.

Granted at install

Internet — INTERNET

What it is for: connecting to the Grid Racer multiplayer server (gridracer.piepgras.info) for online races, account login, browsing and sharing custom tracks, and viewing the rating leaderboard. It is also used to send crash reports to Bugsink, our error-tracking service, when you have turned on crash reporting in Settings (crash reporting is off by default).

How the connection is protected: the connection to our server and the crash reports are encrypted (TLS). A server you set yourself in Settings, such as one on your own home network, may be unencrypted (an address beginning with ws://); the app then warns you in Settings that what you send it, your password included, is readable on the network.

When it is used: the app connects to the server only when you enter the multiplayer race setup screen or the account/track-sharing screens. Local play (single-player, hot-seat, AI races) does not require this permission, and with crash reporting off, the app makes no network requests during local play.

What it does not do: it grants no access to your camera, microphone, location, contacts, or storage outside its own app folder.

info.piepgras.gridracer.DYNAMIC_RECEIVER_NOT_EXPORTED_PERMISSION

What it is: a permission the Android build tools generate for the app and grant only to the app itself, so that receivers registered at runtime cannot be triggered by other apps.

What it is for: internal safety. It grants no access to anything and is not visible to you or to other apps as a capability.

Requested at runtime

Notifications — POST_NOTIFICATIONS

What it is for: letting the app show a "your turn" notification when you are in an online multiplayer race, the app is in the background, and the server is waiting on your move. Online races are turn-based with a per-room time limit set by the room's host; a backgrounded player who never sees the clock running loses their turn to it. Tapping the notification reopens the app to the race.

When it is requested: once, the first time you open the app on Android 13 or newer. Devices on older Android versions grant this automatically and are never prompted.

What happens if you refuse it, or turn it off later in system settings: the app works exactly the same in every other respect. You simply will not get a reminder if you background the app during your turn, and the room's timeout still applies regardless — the notification only helps you notice it in time, it does not change or extend it.

What it does not do: generating this notification involves no new data collection. The app already knows whose turn it is and how much time is left from the race it is already connected to; nothing about it is sent to us or to anyone else to produce the notification, and nothing about the notification itself is stored once it is shown.

Never requested

The app does not request, and cannot access:

  • location, in any form;
  • camera or microphone;
  • contacts, calendar or call history;
  • SMS or phone state;
  • broad file/storage access;
  • exact alarms or wake locks;
  • foreground-service permissions;
  • the advertising identifier — there is no advertising and no tracking.

Only one permission requires your explicit approval — notifications, and only on Android 13 and newer (see above). Every other permission listed here is granted automatically at install and never prompts you.

If this changes

If a future version of Grid Racer needs another permission, this page will be updated to list exactly what was added and why, matching the app's declared permissions at that time.
